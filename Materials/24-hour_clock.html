24-Hour Clock

The 24-hour clock is a method of time notation in which the day runs from midnight to midnight and is divided into 24 hours. Unlike the 12-hour clock, it does not use AM or PM designations. Normally, the hours are represented by two digits, ranging from 00 to 23. So, there is very little evidence of any 24-hour analogue clock usage in everyday life. Thus, digital 24-hour clocks are more commonly used. Still, the 24-hour clock is widely used in military, aviation, and other professional settings.

But in our day-to-day lives, we often encounter the 12-hour clock, which is more familiar and convenient for most people. But one disadvantage of the 12-hour clock is that it can be ambiguous, especially when referring to times later in the day for those who use analogue clocks. For instance, 8 PM is the same as 20:00 in 24-hour format. But if we consider the time 20:00, it is clear that it is 8 PM in the 12-hour format. Otherwise in 12-hour clock format, especially in analogue clocks, it can be confusing. Because 8:00 on an analogue clock could be either 8 AM (in the morning) or 8 PM (at night), depending on the context. So, it is important to understand both formats and their applications.

Mathematical Derivation of Hand Angles for a 24-Hour Clock

In a standard 24-hour clock face, the full circle of \(360^\circ\) is distributed across 24 hours instead of 12. This changes the angular velocity of the hour hand while keeping the minute hand traversal identical.

1. Hour Hand Traversal (\( \theta_H\) ):

  • Hourly rate: Since \(360^\circ / 24 = 15^\circ\) per hour, \(H\) hours contribute \(15H^\circ\).
  • Minute contribution: The hour hand moves \(15^\circ\) over 60 minutes, which is \(15^\circ / 60 = 0.25^\circ\) (or \(\frac{M}{4}\)) per minute.
  • Second contribution: Over 60 seconds, it moves an additional \(0.25^\circ / 60 = \frac{1}{240}^\circ\) per second.

Combining these, the total degrees traversed by the hour hand from the 0/24 position is:

$$ \theta_H = 15H + \frac{M}{4} + \frac{S}{240} $$

2. Minute Hand Traversal (\( \theta_M\) ):

The minute hand completes a full \(360^\circ\) rotation every 60 minutes, regardless of the clock dial's numbering system:

  • Minute rate: \(360^\circ / 60 = 6^\circ\) per minute (\(6M\)).
  • Second contribution: \(6^\circ / 60 = 0.1^\circ\) (or \(\frac{S}{10}\)) per second.

Thus, the total degrees traversed by the minute hand is:

\[ \theta_M = 6M + \frac{S}{10} \]

3. Angle Between Hour and Minute Hands (\( \Delta\theta \) ):

Subtracting the minute hand's position from the hour hand's position yields the absolute angular difference:

$$ \Delta\theta = \left| \left(15H + \frac{M}{4} + \frac{S}{240}\right) - \left(6M + \frac{S}{10}\right) \right| $$

Simplifying the coefficients gives the direct formula:

$$ \Delta\theta = \left| 15H - \frac{23}{4}M - \frac{23}{240}S \right| $$

(Note: If the resulting absolute value exceeds \(180^\circ\), take \(360^\circ - \Delta\theta\) to find the minor interior angle).
